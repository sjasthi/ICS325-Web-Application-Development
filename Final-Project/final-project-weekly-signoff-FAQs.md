# Final Project — Weekly Signoff FAQs

The **Weekly Signoff Sheet** is used to document your individual progress throughout the ICS 499 Final Project.

Template:
https://github.com/sjasthi/ICS325-Web-Application-Development/blob/main/Final-Project/project-weekly-signoff-sheet.txt

The weekly signoff is both:

* a record of your individual contributions, and
* a way for you and the instructor to review your progress from one iteration to the next.

---

## Q1. Should we submit the Weekly Signoff Sheet every week?

**Yes.**

The Weekly Signoff Sheet must be submitted **every week**.

It is due on the **day of class by midnight**.

A grace period of up to **24 hours** is allowed. Submissions beyond the grace period will not be accepted.

The template is available here:

https://github.com/sjasthi/ics499/blob/main/Assets/weekly_signoff.txt

---

## Q2. Should everyone on the team submit a Weekly Signoff?

**Yes.**

Even if you are working on a group project, **each student must submit an individual Weekly Signoff**.

The purpose of the signoff is to document **your individual contribution** to the project.

---

## Q3. Should the Weekly Signoff look the same for everyone on the team?

**No.**

Your Weekly Signoff should reflect:

* what **you personally contributed** during the current iteration,
* any challenges or issues you encountered,
* how your work contributed to the overall project, and
* what you plan to work on during the **next iteration**.

Students on the same team may share common project goals, but their individual contributions and planned work should normally be different.

---

## Q4. How are absences handled?

You are expected to be present in the **breakout room** during class.

The normal weekly process is:

1. Join the breakout room.
2. Provide an update on your work.
3. Demonstrate your progress when appropriate.
4. Receive feedback from the instructor.
5. Submit the updated `weekly_signoff.txt` to the Dropbox after the discussion.

If you are absent, you may not receive full credit for that week's iteration.

The Weekly Signoff by itself does **not** replace participation in the breakout-room review.

## Q7. What format should the Weekly Signoff use?

Use the template provided here:

https://github.com/sjasthi/ICS325-Web-Application-Development/blob/main/Final-Project/project-weekly-signoff-sheet.txt

The file may remain a **plain-text `.txt` file**.

There is no requirement to convert it to Word, PDF, or another format.

---

## Q8. Should we create a brand-new Weekly Signoff file every week?

**No.**

Keep **one `weekly_signoff.txt` file** and continue updating it throughout the semester.

For each new iteration, add the newest section at the **top of the file**.

In other words:

**Latest iteration → oldest iteration**

This creates one continuous record of your progress.

At any point during the semester, you and the instructor should be able to open one file and review the history of your project contributions.

---

## Q9. Can I submit several Weekly Signoffs later as a batch?

**No.**

The Weekly Signoff is intended to document progress **each week**, not retroactively at the end of several iterations.

It is due on the day of class.

You may submit within the **24-hour grace period**.

Submissions beyond that period will not be accepted.

Do not save several weeks of updates and submit them together later.

---

## Q10. I attended the breakout room and gave a demo. Do I still need to submit the Weekly Signoff?

**Yes.**

The breakout-room review/demo and the Weekly Signoff serve different purposes.

The breakout-room session allows you to:

* demonstrate your work,
* discuss progress,
* ask questions, and
* receive instructor feedback.

The Weekly Signoff provides the **written record of your progress**.

Therefore, even if you attended the breakout room and demonstrated your work, you must still submit the Weekly Signoff to the Dropbox.

---

## Q11. What should we submit for the final FP-Handoff?

For the final FP-Handoff, continue using the **same Weekly Signoff file**.

Your final entry should reflect the final state of the project and your final contributions.

You will also submit a **self-evaluation spreadsheet** based on your planned work in:

`FP_iteration_plan.md`

For each planned work item, indicate its final status:

* **Done**
* **Partially Done**
* **Not Done**

The purpose is to compare:

**What you originally planned → What you actually delivered**

For work that is partially done or not done, briefly explain the reason when appropriate.

---

## Q12. Should we submit the project code to the Dropbox?

**No.**

You do **not** need to upload the source code to the Dropbox.

All project source code should be maintained in **GitHub**.

In the final Weekly Signoff, include confirmation that the GitHub remote repository (`origin`) contains the latest version of your project code.

For example:

> I confirmed that the GitHub origin repository contains the latest project code and all changes for this iteration have been pushed.

The Dropbox should contain the required project documentation and signoff materials — not another copy of the GitHub repository.

---

# Weekly Workflow — Recommended Checklist

For every Final Project iteration:

1. Work on your assigned project items during the week.
2. Update your project's GitHub repository regularly.
3. Update your `weekly_signoff.txt`.
4. Add the **latest iteration at the top** of the file.
5. Join the breakout room during class.
6. Explain what you accomplished.
7. Demonstrate your work when appropriate.
8. Discuss problems, risks, and next steps.
9. Receive instructor feedback.
10. Update your plans for the next iteration.
11. Submit the Weekly Signoff to the Dropbox by midnight, or within the 24-hour grace period.

---

## Remember

The Weekly Signoff is not simply an assignment to complete for a grade.

It is your **running record of project progress**.

By the end of the semester, one file should clearly show:

**Plan → Work → Demo → Feedback → Adjustment → Next Iteration → Final Delivery**
